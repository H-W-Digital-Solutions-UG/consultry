# Consultry — The Uncomfortable Review

**Status:** Venture-level critique, requested. Ratifies nothing. Written to be argued with, and I may be wrong.
**Date:** 2026-08-07
**Scope:** Not the artifacts. The venture.

The two prior reviews critiqued the documents. This one critiques the pattern that produced them. It is deliberately harder than the others, because that was the ask.

---

## 0. If I only got one sentence

> **You have spent months building a rigorous, intellectually honest, genuinely impressive body of product definition — and by your own register you still cannot name one external consultancy that wants this, at what price, for which job. The definition work is not preparation for that answer. It has become a substitute for it.**

Everything below is a consequence of that sentence.

---

## 1. The pattern, stated plainly

What exists: roughly 1.1 MB of product definition. Sixty-plus Wayfinder tickets. At least two fully archived prior generations (Gen A; the superseded 2026-08 product baseline). Nine to twenty-six overlapping conceptual frameworks. A ratified coverage ledger, journey portfolio, three reference threads, an experience contract, a 62-item WBS.

What does not exist, by your own documents:
- **G6 — open.** *"konkreter 30-Tage-Plan von ~3 auf 3–5 externe Design Partner."* Your own note on it: *"LinkedIn/Events sind Kanal, nicht Plan."*
- **G7 — open.** ≥1 external existing-client-led design partner, specifically to stop over-fitting to your own firm.
- **Personas** — *"pre-pivot 50–200-person synthetic scenarios."*
- **Every buying-moment claim** in the Evidence Register — rated **Low**.
- **The central claim** — *collective expertise becomes actionable* — rated **"Medium for problem; low for mechanism."** That is the highest-confidence claim you have.
- Your own verdict: *"Own-firm use cannot validate independent willingness to buy, portability across archetypes or external adoption."*

Now the uncomfortable part. **The rigour is what makes this dangerous.** If the definition work were sloppy, you would already have abandoned it. Because it is excellent — because the Evidence Register genuinely refuses to promote unproven claims, because the Coverage Ledger genuinely states its own limits, because you genuinely mark assumptions with † — it *feels* like progress. Every closed ticket is real intellectual work. And none of it moves the number that decides whether this company exists.

This is the most sophisticated form of avoidance I have seen in a product corpus. I don't say that to wound. I say it because you will not recognise it as avoidance from the inside — it doesn't feel like avoidance, it feels like diligence, and the artifacts are good enough to keep confirming that.

**The test:** name the last decision in the corpus that was changed by something an external consultancy said. If you can't, the loop isn't closed.

## 2. The mismatch that could kill this

This is the finding I'd most want you to argue with, because if it's right, it's structural.

**Your differentiation is enterprise-shaped. Your buyer is not.**

Everything that makes Consultry defensible against a horizontal company brain is risk-reduction: client separation, rights and confidentiality review, the Decision-and-Effect Authority Spine, evidence state, provenance, audit events, WC-Mode, AI-Act posture, single-human responsible completion. That list sells to an organisation with a general counsel, a compliance function, a CISO and a procurement process.

Your ratified first-class archetype is a **Partner-led Specialist Boutique, 5–30 consultants, heavily role-compressed.** That partner has no general counsel. They have a laptop, a pipeline, and sixty hours of billable pressure. They do not buy governance. They buy *won mandates* and *hours back*.

And here is the tell: in your corpus, the governance apparatus is specified to the level of nine-cell assessment enums and separately-attributable authority bases — while the things that would actually move that partner (speed, senior leverage, winning more) are the thinnest, most deferred, least fixtured content you have. **You have specified the parts your buyer doesn't buy, to a depth your buyer can't evaluate.**

Three ways out, and you have to pick one:

- **(a) Change the ICP.** Governance sells at 100+ consultants with multi-practice structures and real compliance exposure. But that contradicts the ratified archetype decision, lengthens the sales cycle, and puts you against firms with budgets.
- **(b) Re-weight the differentiation.** Governance becomes the quiet enabler that makes the leverage *possible and safe*, never the headline. The headline becomes senior leverage: your best people's judgement reaching every mandate. This is the option I'd take, and it's what the expertise lever actually gives you.
- **(c) Argue I'm wrong** — that DACH boutiques do buy governance because of Vergaberecht, Mitbestimmung and client confidentiality obligations that bite harder at small scale than I think. **That is an empirical question you could answer in ten conversations.** You have not had them.

Note that (c) being true would be excellent news and would justify much of the corpus. You just don't know, and you've built as if you do.

## 3. The pricing model is on the wrong axis

At €50/seat/month, your ratified first-class archetype produces:

| Firm size | Annual contract value |
|---|---|
| 5 consultants | €3,000 |
| 15 consultants | €9,000 |
| 30 consultants | **€18,000** |

Your stated ACV band is **€15–45k**. The top of your first-class archetype barely reaches the bottom of your ACV band. To hit €45k you need **75 seats** — which is the *second* archetype, and near the top of it.

**Your pricing assumes the customer you deprioritised.** Either the ACV band is wrong, the archetype is wrong, or the price is wrong. All three are ratified simultaneously and they don't reconcile.

There's a deeper problem. You are pricing per head for a product whose value is explicitly firm-level and compounding: *"macht ihre kollektive Expertise und ihr Firmenwissen handlungswirksam."* Corpus compounding, reuse, blind-spot coverage, cross-project symbiosis — none of these scale with headcount, they scale with *knowledge density and project volume*. Worse, your own anti-people-scoring invariants mean **you cannot measure or demonstrate per-seat value even in principle.** You've committed to a pricing axis you've forbidden yourself to instrument.

And the comparison is now live: a horizontal company brain sells at $199/month for unlimited members. A 20-person boutique sees €12,000/yr against ~$2,400/yr. You can win that comparison — but only on arguments you have not yet written down, to a buyer you have not yet met.

## 4. Founder-market-fit has become a hiding place

Design partner #0 being your own cybersecurity consultancy is a genuine asset. It's also the ideal environment for building something only you want, and your own documents already see the risk:

> The showcase demo is **tender-led** (cybersecurity → BSI/KRITIS/public sector), while the GTM sequence for mid-to-small firms is **Bestandskunden-led**. Guardrail against *"Consultry für die eigene Firma."*

That tension was identified and has been open since. Meanwhile the entire ERP reference thread — the strongest, most visceral asset in the corpus — is **fictional**. You built a fictional case for the motion you believe in, and a real case for the motion you're not selling.

A consultancy founder building consultancy software has one enormous advantage: you can run the concierge version tomorrow. You could sell three DACH boutiques *"we will do your knowledge-to-action work manually for €2k/month"* and learn more about willingness-to-pay in six weeks than the click dummy will ever tell you. You have the credibility, the network and the domain language to do this. **It is the highest-information, lowest-cost experiment available to you and it appears nowhere in the plan.**

## 5. Your moat arguments are weaker than you think

Four are written down. Graded honestly:

| Claim | My read |
|---|---|
| *"AI-native beats AI-bolted-on; the intelligence sits between the layers"* | True and unownable. Everyone says this. It's a category argument, not a moat. |
| *"Beratung im KI-Zeitalter"* market turn | Real tailwind, benefits every entrant equally. Tailwinds are not moats. |
| *"DACH-spezifisch ist ein Moat, kein Nachteil"* | **Weakest of the four.** These are features — German depth, Vergaberecht, Mitbestimmung, EU posture — and a global player answers them with one German PM and one compliance lawyer. Your own register also notes no Swiss primary source exists, so even the word *DACH* overstates your evidence. |
| **Corpus compounding → switching cost** | **The only real one.** And it requires customers with corpora. You have none. |

So your single genuine moat is currently at zero, and its strength is a direct function of the thing you've been deferring. Every month spent in definition is a month the moat doesn't start compounding. That is the actual cost of the delay, and it's not linear — compounding moats punish late starts disproportionately.

**Sharper version:** a competitor with worse thinking and ten paying DACH boutiques has a bigger moat than you do today, and the gap widens monthly.

## 6. The gate structure is a machine for not shipping

Look at what your own DAG does to your most important question. `define-knowledge-to-action-and-blind-spot-finding-contracts` — the contract for the mechanism you named as the product's core — is blocked by *Ratify Core Continuity Obligations*, which is blocked by *Prototype the Systemic Click Dummy*, which is open. It sits **four gates deep behind a prototype your own gate ticket says cannot validate product-market fit.**

Meanwhile the sequence is: Click Dummy → Three-Slice Contract → Gap Register → Definition-complete Handoff → Technical PoC → *then* a separate Full-Backend-MVP decision. Six gates before a line of production code, each one requiring definition completeness, none requiring a customer.

**Every gate in this structure is a definition gate. Not one is an evidence gate.** That is why the structure cannot terminate: definition completeness is always achievable with more thinking, so the gate always eventually opens, and the process never encounters a wall that forces a decision about reality.

Add exactly one gate and the whole machine changes shape: **no Technical PoC until three external consultancies have committed money or scheduled time.** That single constraint reorders everything downstream of it, and it costs nothing to adopt.

## 7. You may have become too abstract to sell

Read your own vocabulary back: Coverage Views, Reference Threads, Cross-Cutting Contracts, Authority Spines, Disposition, Materiality, Claim Ceiling, Scope Trace, Responsibility Assignment, Effect Admission.

Now read what a managing partner says when they're unhappy: *"we lost the Stadtwerke tender because nobody found the 2023 reference in time."* *"Markus is the only one who knows the migration methodology and he's booked until March."* *"I spent Sunday rewriting a junior's deck."*

There is almost no language in 1.1 MB at that second altitude. The one place you have it — *"Sie sind Berater. Kein Daten-Pfleger."* — is filed as a persona line. Your best customer-facing sentence is a footnote.

The abstraction level of your thinking has become the abstraction level of your product. That's why the click dummy validates semantics rather than value: the spec was written in the register the corpus is fluent in, and value doesn't live there. **You cannot sell a Cross-Cutting Contract. You can sell "Markus's judgement is available when Markus isn't."**

## 8. What I would do in the next 30 days

Aggressive on purpose. Adjust for what you know that I don't.

**Week 1 — stop and re-aim.**
- Freeze the click dummy build at its current scope. Do not start the 62-item WBS.
- Write the three falsification sentences from the previous review. If you cannot write a condition under which you would abandon this direction, you are not running an experiment.
- Extract the single strongest scene — the ERP Wave-1 / Reconciliation v3 challenge — and nothing else.

**Week 2 — build the smallest thing that creates desire.**
- Ten screens. Figma or hand-written HTML. One week, not twelve. Its job is not to validate your ontology; its job is to make a partner say *"wait, go back — can it really do that?"*
- Build the firm-knowledge fixture: one named method, one named proof, one named prior decision with its rationale, one Project Nord overlap with a stated non-transferable part. **This is the content that makes the demo real, and it is writing, not engineering.**

**Weeks 2–4 — go outside, in parallel.**
- Twelve conversations with DACH boutique and growing-specialist partners. Not demos. Interviews first, demo second.
- Three questions, and only these three: *What did you lose in the last twelve months, and why? Who is your bottleneck person and what happens when they're unavailable? What do you already pay for that touches this?*
- Then the demo, then the only question that matters: **"What would you pay to have this in September, and what would you need to see first?"**
- In parallel, pitch the concierge version to three of them. €2k/month, you do it by hand. A yes is worth more than the entire click dummy. A no tells you faster than six more gates.

**End of month — decide against a number, not a feeling.**
- ≥3 firms scheduling paid pilots → the definition corpus was worth it; proceed to the Technical PoC with real fixtures.
- 0–1 firms → the problem is not that the definition is incomplete. Change the wedge, the archetype, or the pricing axis. **Do not respond by defining more.** That is the failure mode this document exists to name.

## 9. Where I might be wrong

I owe you this, because I have exactly as much market evidence as you do — none.

- **DACH SME consulting may genuinely buy governance.** Vergaberecht, Mitbestimmung and client confidentiality may bite harder at 15 people than my instinct says. If so, §2 is wrong and much of the corpus is vindicated.
- **The pivot may have been necessary.** Going from Opportunity-to-Concept to the whole-product view looks like real learning, not drift. Some of the definition volume bought you a correct scope you'd otherwise have discovered expensively in code.
- **I may be underrating the compounding of the corpus itself.** When you do reach a technical PoC, you'll move faster than a team that skipped this. That's real — it's just not worth much if the destination is wrong.
- **You may already be having these conversations** and simply not writing them into the repo. If so, ignore §1 and write them down — an evidence log is worth more than another contract.
- **The Krallmann work** is described as parallel commercial evidence. I have not seen it. If it contains real willingness-to-pay signal, it changes the picture and belongs in the Evidence Register, not beside it.

## 10. The part that isn't criticism

I want to be precise about what's actually good here, because it matters for what you do next.

The thinking in this corpus is better than the thinking in most funded seed-stage companies. The Evidence Register's refusal to promote its own claims, the Coverage Ledger stating its own limits, the discipline of marking assumptions with †, the willingness to write *"these are intended measured outcomes, not observed facts"* about your own moat — that is rare, and it's the reason this review could be written at all. Most founders' documents cannot be audited because they contain nothing falsifiable. Yours can.

The `Observation → Signal → human decision → ActionCase` cut is a real product insight. The separation of evidential assessment from current materiality is a genuine contribution. `ProjectStatusAssessment` is the best object in the corpus. The refusal to fabricate — *"no chart is better than a fabricated one"* — is the instinct that will make this product trustworthy if it ships.

**None of that is the problem. The problem is that all of it is pointed inward.** You have built an extraordinary instrument for reasoning about a market you have not yet touched.

Point it outward. The same rigour, aimed at twelve conversations and three paid pilots, would give you in one month what the last three have not.
