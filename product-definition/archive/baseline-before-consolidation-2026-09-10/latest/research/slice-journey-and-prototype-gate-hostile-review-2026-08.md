# Consultry — Hostile Review of the Slice Cut, the Journey Portfolio and the Click Dummy Gate

**Status:** Adversarial review. Ratifies nothing. Written to be argued with.
**Date:** 2026-08-07
**Method:** Three independent deep reads of the ratified corpus — slice architecture, prototype scope, knowledge/reuse lever — plus the Evidence Register as the falsification lens.
**Trigger:** the Agently review exposed an un-named human-expertise claim; the founder asked whether the slices and journeys are smartly cut.

---

## 0. The one diagnosis

Everything below reduces to a single failure mode, and it is not sloppiness — it is the shadow side of a genuine strength.

> **The definition process has been optimising for coherence rather than for consequence.**

Each artifact has been judged by *"is this internally consistent, non-contradictory, and does it avoid overclaiming?"* — a bar this corpus clears better than most funded startups. Almost nothing has been judged by *"does this change what we build, or what we would learn?"*

That single bias generates every symptom found independently by three reviews:

| Symptom | Coherence explanation |
|---|---|
| Slices cut by lifecycle phase, not by mechanism | The three slices are the top three rows of the Coverage Ledger table. Coherent selection; no mechanism analysis is recorded anywhere. |
| Nine to twenty-six overlapping taxonomies | Each is individually coherent. None was ever asked to justify a decision it alone changes. |
| Prototype Definition of Done tests semantics, not value | Semantic conformance is checkable without evidence. Value is not. |
| Governance chains fully specified, the value they gate deferred | Gates can be specified from an armchair. Benefits cannot. |
| 85% mutation coverage on a throwaway mock | Internal consistency of props, enforced by tooling. |
| The consultant is an input in every flow | A beneficiary is a *consequence* claim. The corpus grew in the only direction it could grow without evidence. |

That last row is the one that matters. **The reason the human-expertise lever keeps failing to land is that it is the one claim that cannot be settled by more definition.** It is not missing because it was rejected. It is missing because the process has no way to produce it.

The corrective is not more rigour. It is a different question at each gate: *what would have to be false for this to be wrong, and what does this change?*

**State the strengths plainly, because the critique depends on them being real.** The Evidence Register's S/I/H/U grading and its refusal to promote claims; the Coverage Ledger's explicit statement of its own limits; the three depth classes (`Reference now` / `Canonical later` / `Horizon contract`) as a genuine anti-scope-creep device; `Observation → Signal → human decision → ActionCase` with only the commercial branch becoming an `Opportunity`; the refusal to fabricate ("no chart is better than a fabricated one"); negative paths as first-class results. This is better epistemic hygiene than almost any pre-seed product definition. The problem is not the quality of the thinking. It is where the thinking has been pointed.

## 1. The lever, stated properly

The founder's formulation — *the consultant is where knowledge gets connected, used and reused* — is not a new idea to be inserted. **It is already ratified, at the top of the outcome hierarchy, and then de-operationalised at every level below it.**

From `ratify-the-product-problem-and-outcome-hierarchy.md`:

> "Der gemeinsame unmittelbare **User Outcome und Wirkmechanismus** ist die **Expert Knowledge-to-Action Bridge**: Verantwortliche Rollen erhalten **direkten, kontextbezogenen Zugang** zum relevanten Expertenwissen, zu Erfahrung, Artefakten und Begründungen."

That is the lever, ratified, in the governing decision. Nothing needs reopening. Everything downstream needs to be made to obey it.

### 1.1 The three verbs, and what each is missing

**CONNECT — firm knowledge reaches this person in this situation.**
Specified once, correctly, as Coverage Ledger `KR-1`: *"A concrete job needs firm knowledge, source evidence, a method or direct expert input."* Its objects — `Expert Request/Participation`, `Contextual Answer/Work Input` — appear **nowhere else in the entire corpus**. No data model, no UX frame, no thread, no anchor. Its designated follow-up thread opens by disclaiming it: *"This is not: a complete Knowledge/Reuse Journey."* Its journey depth is `Canonical later`. Its ticket is open behind four open dependencies. The UX Operating Model explicitly denies it a surface: *"Immer verfügbar, aber **nicht als eigener Modulweg**: globale Suche / „Ask Consultry"."*

The product's named core mechanism has zero surfaces.

**USE — applied in real work, with the consultant's judgement.**
Every one of the ten co-work moments terminates in a governance state. Read the state-consequence column end to end: *"attention disposition only"*, *"corrected Working Context"*, *"Goal/Work Brief draft; not Approval"*, *"exact Draft/Plan version changes"*, *"Evidence State and Claim Ceiling update"*, *"human disposition; still no Effect"*. Ten moments, ten state transitions, **not one of them is "the consultant now has a thing they would otherwise have spent two hours making."** Even artifact production resolves to *version state changed*, not *artifact is better*.

Every scripted scene is exception-triggered — a signal, a contradiction, a conflict. Nothing covers building the workshop deck on an ordinary Tuesday. The ticket that names this risk (`define-the-active-client-work-journey-anatomy…`) is open, and is blocked by the very thread that caused the reduction.

**REUSE — their judgement becomes available to the next person.**
Seven human gates before any consultant sees a released asset; ten before a value case exists. And gates 3–7 plus 9 all land on the Knowledge/Practice Owner, the Contract/Governance Reviewer and Finance — **who, in a Partner-led Specialist Boutique, are the same overloaded senior whose overload is the ratified buying moment.** The governance chain is heaviest exactly where the ratified entry buyer has no capacity. Nothing in the corpus acknowledges this.

### 1.2 The conflation that costs the most

**Light reuse and heavy reuse are different products, and the corpus treats them as one pipeline.**

- **Light reuse** — *"this resembles what we did at Project Nord; here is who worked on it; here is what they decided and why; here are the sources."* Internal, within-tenant, within an already-permitted context. **Requires no abstraction, no de-identification, no rights review.** It is a retrieval and attribution capability.
- **Heavy reuse** — cross-client, externalised, productised assets. Genuinely needs the whole chain: abstraction, de-identification, IP/confidentiality/usage-rights review, versioned release.

The distinction exists in embryo in four places and is never named:

- Coverage Ledger separates `KR-1` (knowledge/expert need) from `KR-2` (governed reuse) — this is exactly the split;
- Journey Portfolio JF-3: *"Direct application and reuse/productization are **related variants, not one forced linear path**"*;
- Active-Project thread §12 orders coordination **before** productization: *"human validation and current-work coordination → **optional** ReuseCandidate"*;
- CoWork Moment Map §6, six words that are the whole thesis: *"**current Project work is not blocked by productization**."*

And then it is overridden. The Knowledge/Reuse thread §13.2 notices two different origins, calls them *"already distinguished"*, and routes both into the identical heavy pipeline. The Symbiosis Graph has exactly one path; its only fast exit from pattern detection goes **to Sales**, not to the consultant.

**Why this is the expensive error:** it puts every unit of knowledge value behind a governance wall whose payoff is distant, whose gates land on the busiest person in the firm, and whose end state — `ReuseValueCase` — is required by invariant to strip out *whose* expertise produced the value. Expertise becomes measurable in aggregate and unattributable in particular: precisely the shape of an asset that cannot be demonstrated.

**The permission for light reuse already exists.** `ReuseScope {ProjectOnly, AccountOnly, TenantInternalAbstracted, …}` is ratified, and the hard invariants restrict only *cross-account* reuse. The `ReusableAsset` boundary decision keeps content in `KnowledgeAssetVersion` and `SkillRelease` rather than copying it — so a light-reuse capability can be built on `KnowledgeAssetVersion` + `SymbiosisLink` + provenance **without reopening a single ratified decision.** The door is open. Nobody has walked through it.

### 1.3 The primitive that solves this, already ratified

`ProjectStatusAssessment` is the best object in the corpus and it is used once.

> "`ProjectStatusSnapshot` bleibt rebuildbare Faktenprojektion; **verantwortete menschliche Einschätzungen leben getrennt als nachvollziehbare `ProjectStatusAssessment`s**."

Human judgement, first-class, attributed, versioned, carrying its rationale and its basis, kept separate from computed facts, never silently overwriting them. That is *exactly* the shape required to make a senior's judgement reusable without the senior present.

Generalise it — a responsible person's reasoned judgement **on any subject**, retained with author, time, rationale and basis, retrievable by the next person who faces the same question — and one move simultaneously answers:

- the **senior bottleneck** (named five times in the corpus as a flag, never once as a mechanism; the journey that would address it, JF-4, is parked as `Horizon contract`);
- **light reuse** (a decision plus its rationale is the highest-value reusable unit in a consultancy, and it needs no rights review to circulate internally);
- **expertise as a visible asset** (attribution without ranking — the anti-scoring rules forbid *comparison between persons*, not *provenance*);
- and the **CONNECT verb** (what a consultant actually wants is rarely a document; it is "what did we decide, and why").

It requires no new governance, because the governance is already ratified for project status.

### 1.4 The anti-scoring rules are correct — and nothing was ever written on the other side of them

GI-9, GI-12, GI-13, plus `"scoring_allowed": false` and `"visibility": "profile-owner-and-aggregated-bid-use"`. These forbid: comparison between persons, performance scoring, hidden ranking, un-gated person-sharp external use. They are right and should not be touched.

They do **not** forbid: a person seeing their own evidence (the visibility flag already contemplates it); attributed provenance on a reused asset (`owner` and `source_lineage[]` already exist); telling a consultant that a decision they made was reused; consented, purpose-bound expert credit.

The consultant's only named benefit from Capability Evidence today is *"you have to update your CV less"* (GI-12a auto-maintained profiles). That is the whole of it.

`separate-the-core-skill-graph-from-capability-evidence.md` closed the semantic question — Skill Graph ≠ human capability — and left the product question entirely unasked: **what is Capability Evidence for, and who benefits from it?** The resolution names no beneficiary.

## 2. The slice cut is wrong in a specific, fixable way

### 2.1 It is a lifecycle cut wearing a mechanism costume

The three slices are `Win & Commit` → `Deliver & Assure` → `Use, Learn & Reuse`: the first three rows of the Coverage Ledger, in order, one each. No alternative cut is recorded as considered and rejected.

The consequence is that the same mechanism is demonstrated three times. Strip the nouns from the three Human-AI tables and you get one function: **bind context → retrieve and compare sources → surface conflict or gap → propose candidates → human disposes → controlled effect.** Slice 2's own text concedes the pattern is transferable. Slice 1 §11 specifies the three alignment lenses that Slice 3 §5 specifies again. Slices 2 and 3 share the same fixture, the same client, the same artifact, the same version, the same conflict.

A design partner sees: *Tender criteria matrix* → *Evidence conflict matrix* → *Alignment concern matrix*. The demo's own probe list worries about fragmentation — *"does this feel like one Consulting OS or three tools joined for a demo?"* The actual risk is the opposite. **Three costumes, one act.**

Three genuine mechanism differences do exist — and none of them is the stated slice boundary:

1. **Slice 2 is the only system-initiated slice** — the only place the product must *earn an interruption*, with a distinct failure mode (false positives) and a distinct cost (*"treat review load as Product cost"*). This is a real axis.
2. **Slice 1 is the only slice crossing an external commercial authority boundary**, and the only one with two structurally different source-authority regimes (self-authored Observation vs. externally authoritative Tender). Real, but narrower.
3. **Cross-object compounding is the only mechanism no generic tool can replicate** — and it is defined in Slice 2, staged in Slice 3, and owned by neither.

### 2.2 The moat is last, optional, and truncated

Scene `C3` — cross-project overlap — is the tenth of ten scenes, and the spec disarms it in writing: *"It is not a fourth main Slice, and **completion of the artifact flow never requires it**."* It will be the scene that gets skipped when the session runs long.

Worse, it stops at `ReuseCandidate`. The prototype demonstrates *proposing* a candidate; it never demonstrates *using* a reused asset. The Evidence Register's own experiment design says this is precisely the wrong stopping point: *"Observe an **actual Reuse Application** and its adaptation, not just a recommendation."*

This is a regression. The superseded UX Operating Model §8.3 already had the complete chain through `ReuseApplication` ("this asset fits at 82%, here is the adaptation needed, here are the constraints"), `ServiceBundleCandidate` and `ReuseValueCase`. The ratified Reference Thread amputated steps 5–8 and kept 1–3. **The older document demos better value than the newer ratified one.**

And there is no value acceptance criterion for compounding anywhere. Slice 3's Acceptance Intent asks only whether reuse follow-ups *"remain responsible rather than automatic."* **The moat's only acceptance criterion in the entire ratified definition is a safety criterion.**

### 2.3 Slice 3 is two propositions weakly joined

It bundles the compounding moat with Corporate Artifact Alignment. The bundling is never argued — the thread contains only statements of *separation* between them. CAA lives in Slice 3 because a cross-cutting contract needed a concrete host and Slice 3 was the one being written. That is document logic, not product logic — and CAA is *already* specified in Slice 1 §11 with identical lenses.

Stage-time arithmetic: alignment gets 2 of 10 scenes; compounding gets 1, marked optional. **Hygiene gets double the stage time of the moat, and the moat is the cuttable one.**

The three lenses are also wrongly levelled. Brand/CD is genuine hygiene — the doc admits its ceiling (*"visual conformity does not establish knowledge correctness or permission to release"*); a template checker does this. Governance/Release is not hygiene at all — it is the **precondition of reuse** (confidentiality, IP, usage rights), i.e. the thing that makes cross-client compounding legal rather than merely tidy.

### 2.4 Slice 1 has no fixture, and it shows

Slice 2 names `Decision Basis v7`, `Reconciliation Report v2/v3`, the governing criterion, a 48-hour horizon. Slice 1's equivalent is: *"The exact contracting authority, scope, Lot, dates, criteria weighting and contract instrument remain **fictional scenario details until a real Tender is selected**."* Its required behaviour is six abstract verbs, not one of which names a firm-specific input.

Slice 2 will demo as specific; Slice 1 will demo as generic — not because the job is weaker, but because nobody built the fixture. Participants will read that asymmetry as a judgement about the product.

### 2.5 The worst fixture gap: there is no firm knowledge

Slice 3's premise is applying *"approved Target-Consultancy Knowledge, Method, Terminology and Proof."* Nowhere in the document is there **one named method, one named proof, one example approved claim.** Compare two adjacent rows of the same fixture table:

> "Material conflict | `Reconciliation Report v3` no longer supports the existing unqualified Go claim based on `v2`."
> "Firm basis | Applicable approved Target-Consultancy Knowledge, Method, Terminology and Proof."

One is a named, versioned, checkable object. The other is a category label. The compounding payload is no better: `Project Nord` *"**may contain** an overlapping solution pattern."*

**For the thread named after the Knowledge-to-Action Bridge, the knowledge is a placeholder.** A design partner will ask "what does it actually know?" and there is no fixture to answer with.

## 3. Where you are over-defined

Nine to twenty-six overlapping vocabularies, depending on how you count, for a prototype with ten scenes:

Business Coverage Views (6+1) · Journey Families (6+1) · depth classes (3) · UX modes (7) · consulting-work primitives (17–18) · Interaction Frames (12) · surface hypotheses (4) · core Product surfaces (7) · moment archetypes (10) · disclosure levels (4) · dynamic block families (6 or 8) · capability modules (8) · role workspaces (7) · cross-cutting contracts (5) · trust taxonomies (4) · anti-pattern lists (3, overlapping).

Two tells that this has passed the point of usefulness:

- **Every layer is defined primarily by negation.** *"Journey Families are not modules, navigation areas, fixed roles, screens, Agents, graphs, technical workflows…"* · *"The modes are neither Journey Families, modules, screens nor a mandatory sequence"* · *"a coverage and design checklist, not a user-facing taxonomy."* A taxonomy that needs four sentences of disclaimer per definition has outgrown its decision value.
- **Live contradictions between "ratified" documents dated one day apart.** Four surface families vs. seven core surfaces. Six block families vs. eight. Frames F0–F11 vs. the same concepts under different names. Slice 2 ratifies two three-valued enums; Slice 3 explicitly refuses to ratify exactly that class of decision. Nobody has stated which governs.

**Droppable without changing anything the prototype proves:** the 7 UX modes (map to nothing rendered), the 17 work primitives (self-described as an internal checklist), the 8 capability modules (self-described as *"keine Empfehlung für neun gleichrangige Navigationseinträge"*), 5 of the 7 role workspaces (the prototype tests two projections, not seven roles), the 12 Interaction Frames (subsumed by the 7 surfaces; keeping both guarantees drift), three of the four trust taxonomies (Outcome Test / Evidence State / Testability Profile / Claim Ceiling are four names for *what may you responsibly claim right now*), and two of the three question grammars.

## 4. Where you are missing value

### 4.1 The prototype validates its own ontology

Of the 15 Definition-of-Done items, **zero** require the prototype to show that a consultancy's work gets better, faster or more profitable. "Value" appears once — as a capture category for the facilitator's notes. Across ~43 enumerated acceptance and validation criteria in the governing documents, about **3 test whether a consultancy would be better off. Roughly 7%.**

The lexical profile confirms it. Across the four primary prototype specs: `Responsib*` 98 · `Effect` 57 · `Evidence` 56 · `Handoff` 42 · `Authority` 24 — against `value` 8 · `improve` 6 · `faster` **1** · `better` **1** · `cheaper` **0** · `revenue` **0**. The single `faster` is inside the sharpest sentence in the corpus, which is an *anti*-value question: *"Does the visual improve a decision or Work Result, or would plain text have been faster?"*

This is not a value test. It is a self-consistency check on an ontology the team authored, presented as user research.

### 4.2 There is no falsification condition anywhere

A sweep for `falsif|widerleg|abbruch|stop criteri|kill criteri|pivot|scheiter|erfolgskriteri` across all seven prototype documents returns **one** hit, and it is in the superseded v0.1 handover: *"welche Annahmen werden widerlegt?"* — a question to record, not a threshold, not a gate, not a consequence.

What exists instead: 12 open-ended questions with no pass mark; 15 DoD items gating whether the artifact is *ready to be tested* rather than whether the test succeeded; explicit deferral of quantitative thresholds.

The escape hatch is explicit: *"Product Definition remains open until observed user behavior **validates or revises** the hypotheses."* Validate, or revise. There is no third branch in which the direction is abandoned.

And the feedback taxonomy has a hole where the important answer goes. Findings sort into four buckets: prototype correction, product gap, technical question, **commercial evidence need**. Buckets 1–3 all route forward into more building. Bucket 4 — where *"he wasn't excited"* lands — has **no defined consequence**, gates nothing, and does not appear in the handoff criteria.

Meanwhile the gate ticket disclaims the ability to answer the question that would stop the project: *"die Mock-Demo ist weder Backend-Spezifikation noch **Product-/Market-Validation**."*

**A ~40,000-LOC gate, sitting before every technical decision, that the specification itself certifies cannot tell you whether anyone wants this.**

### 4.3 The scope grew 30% in 24 hours, and the growth was invisible-facing

WBS items 49 → 62. Stated LOC ceilings 31,420 → 40,850 (**+9,430**). The single clearest event: the model-composed dynamic surface was *"an optional experiment after the core scenes are understandable, **not** a prerequisite for prototype validation"* on 05.08. On 06.08 it became *"**mandatory** prototype hypotheses, not optional late spikes."* Newly-mandatory work introduced by that delta accounts for ~94% of the growth.

The critique that prompted the change was correct — a ten-scene rail *would* have felt like a slide deck. But the fix chosen was maximal, not minimal. The minimal fix is: keep the ten scenes, remove the rail, let the user click freely, and give `My Work` twelve items instead of three. That is roughly 1,500 LOC, not 9,430.

The tell is a WBS item whose entire purpose is *"honest shallow-state treatment for unimplemented depth"* — infrastructure to gracefully admit that most of what was just added to scope is not really there. That is completeness-seeking with a conscience, not scope discipline.

Roughly **7,000 LOC (~17% of the build)** targets things a design partner cannot perceive: the child-surface architectural rule, the four-level disclosure ladder as a contract, the Operations and Commercials shallow projections, the Boutique/Growing dual projection (a 15-person firm is one of the two, not both), the optional Harness — built in order to certify that it is unnecessary — and facilitator controls.

### 4.4 Engineering rigour aimed at fictional invariants

Mutation testing at 80–85%, *"kein überlebender Mutant in Authority-, Responsibility-, Source- oder Effect-Invarianten"*, visual regression across four viewports, an MSW layer asserting parity with an in-memory fake, every gate proven by deliberate failure — on a **non-functional mock** that will be shown to a handful of people and thrown away.

Mutation testing exists to protect invariants under future change by people who were not there. These invariants are props. If a guard is wrong, the failure mode is a mock displaying a wrong label — which a moderated session catches immediately, for free, and which is *itself a finding*.

Proportionate: typecheck, lint, a handful of tests on the state reducer, one Playwright happy path, and **keep the accessibility gate** — German labels at 200% zoom will genuinely break the layout in front of a real partner.

### 4.5 Nobody's Tuesday gets better

The gap that ties all of this together. There is no scene anywhere in seven documents in which a senior consultant opens Consultry at 16:00 with a half-written workshop deck and leaves at 16:20 with a materially better deck. No decision. No disposition. No handoff. No Claim Ceiling. Just: the work is further along and the person is relieved.

Also absent: cold start (zero substantive hits for onboarding/corpus/setup — the partner's first question, *"what do I feed it and how long until it's useful?"*, has no surface); any economic frame (zero mentions of price, seats, or what Consultry replaces); the status quo (nothing shows what the consultant does today, so "improvement of Work Results" has no referent); realistic volume (`My Work` with three items proves nothing about triage, which is the entire premise of `My Work`).

### 4.6 The one journey about an individual is definitionally demoted

`Personal Daily Attention and Capture` meets the portfolio's own definition of a Journey Family — trigger, progress, disposition, completion state, carry-forward, all specified in a single sentence — and sits in the "horizontal patterns" section instead. Its ticket is **open, unassigned, blocked by two others.**

It is also the prototype's first screen. Definition-of-Done item #1: *"it opens in a believable Consultry workspace rather than a Scene list."* First core surface: *"System Home / My Work — what needs my attention or continued work, and why now?"*

**The single screen every participant sees first, in every session, belongs to an unspecified journey.** And it is the only daily-repeated journey, while the retention claim is daily — a claim the Evidence Register grades *"low confidence for retention."*

The counterargument is fair: promoting it would violate the rule that each family changes a distinct *business progress*, and personal attention changes none by itself. **That objection reveals the root error.** The portfolio uses one taxonomy for two incompatible purposes: a map of business progress, and a selection of validation targets. Prototype slices should be chosen by *what must be tested*, not by *what changes business progress*. Separate the two lists and both answers are correct simultaneously — and the same conflation explains the lifecycle-shaped slice cut.

## 5. What I would actually do

Ranked. Note that the net effect is **less scope, not more.**

### 5.1 Split the two lists (free, unblocks everything)

Business-progress taxonomy stays as ratified. Create a separate, explicit **validation-target list** chosen by *what must be tested*. This is a one-page decision that resolves the Personal-Daily-Attention contradiction, the slice-cut problem and the framework proliferation in one move, without revising a single closed decision.

### 5.2 Make the consultant loop the opening arc, not a fourth slice

Do not add a slice. **Re-frame the demo's first ten minutes** so the prototype opens and closes on the same person:

- **Open:** a consultant, in their own work, receives firm knowledge they did not know existed — a governed asset originating in `Project Nord`, with its lineage, its applicability and its exclusions visible, plus *who decided this and why*. Compounding becomes felt **input at minute three**, not an optional closing scene.
- **Close:** that same consultant's own reasoned judgement becomes available to the next person — light reuse, no rights review, no productization.

The arc is the entire thesis, uses only ratified material, and converts the moat from an epilogue into the frame. It is also the cheapest high-value change available.

### 5.3 Ratify the light/heavy reuse split

Promote four sentences that already exist into one contract:

> **Light reuse** is retrieval and attribution within an already-permitted context: what was decided, by whom, on what basis, and what did not transfer. It requires no abstraction, no de-identification and no rights review, and it must never be blocked by productization.
> **Heavy reuse** is cross-client externalisation and productization. It requires the full governed chain.

This reopens nothing — `ReuseScope` already permits it, the `ReusableAsset` boundary already keeps content in `KnowledgeAssetVersion`, and the Active-Project thread already orders coordination before productization. It simply stops the heavy chain from being the only path.

### 5.4 Generalise `ProjectStatusAssessment` into a first-class expert judgement object

One move; four problems solved (senior bottleneck, light reuse, expertise-as-asset, the CONNECT verb). Already-ratified governance. Attribution without ranking, which the anti-scoring rules permit.

### 5.5 Build the firm-knowledge fixture before the mock

One named method. One named proof. One named approved claim. One concrete `Project Nord` overlap payload with a stated non-transferable part. Without this, Slice 3 demos an empty corpus and the Knowledge-to-Action Bridge is untested in the thread named after it. **This is the highest-value pre-build task and it is content work, not engineering.**

### 5.6 Cut, in this order

- Brand/CD alignment → a passive badge, not a scene. Merge Knowledge + Governance/Release into the reuse path as its enabling conditions.
- Slice 1 → demote to a fixture/branch until it has a real artifact. It is currently the most commodity content in the demo and will be judged as representative.
- Extend the reuse chain forward to an actual `ReuseApplication` (restore what UX OM §8.3 already had).
- Drop the ~7,000 LOC of imperceptible features and the ~1,750 LOC of gate machinery. Keep accessibility.
- Drop Stryker, visual regression and the MSW parity layer.
- Retire the droppable taxonomies from §3 and resolve the two live contradictions (4 vs 7 surfaces; enums ratified vs enums refused).

### 5.7 Write the falsification condition before the first session

Three sentences, and they are the most important sentences in the gate:

1. What specific reaction from a design partner would mean **this direction is wrong**, not merely that the prototype needs correction?
2. What happens to bucket 4 findings — does *"commercial evidence need"* gate the Technical PoC Handoff, or not?
3. What is the false-positive tolerance for Slice 2's challenge detection, and at what rate is the mechanism abandoned rather than tuned?

Without these, the gate cannot say no, and a gate that cannot say no is a schedule, not a gate.

### 5.8 State the mock's honest limit to every participant

Slice 2's value is 100% conditional on **detection**, and detection is the deferred part. In a mock the finding is seeded. The mock proves a finding is comprehensible and actionable — not that it would ever have appeared. Say this out loud in the session, or a *"yes, that's useful"* is worth nothing.

**Genuinely contradicted — need explicit revision:**

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| The Krake wedge and its ten-step canonical journey | Unidirectional by construction; the consultant appears in steps 1–2 and never returns. The lever requires a return leg. This is the GTM door-opener, so it is commercially load-bearing. |
| Journey Portfolio §5 — *"Search/Ask is not a standalone Journey"* | If knowledge-seeking is the primary job, it needs a `Reference now` specification. Also strains the fixed three Deep Anchors. |
| Knowledge/Reuse thread scope (§1, §2, §13.2) | The `Reference now` thread for JF-3 does not exercise JF-3's headline job, and §13.2 funnels light and heavy reuse into one pipeline. |
| `specify-personal-daily-attention-and-capture-journey` — *"kein Blocker"* | It is the prototype's first screen and the only daily-repeated journey. |
| Symbiosis §15 surface allocation | 6 of 7 surfaces are management-facing; `SymbiosisLink.team_solves_same_requirement` already models *"who else is working on this"* and is routed to a lead's review queue instead of to the person who needs it. |

**Already correct — promote, do not reopen:** the ratified outcome hierarchy and the Expert Knowledge-to-Action Bridge (this is your warrant for everything above); Coverage Ledger `KR-1` and `DA-1`; JF-3's *"related variants, not one forced linear path"*; Active-Project §12's coordination-before-productization ordering; CoWork Moment Map §6's *"current Project work is not blocked by productization"*; the `ReusableAsset` boundary.

**A silence, not a contradiction, but a real obstacle:** the Product Actor Ontology has `ResponsibilityDefinition`, `ResponsibilityAssignment`, `CaseParticipation` and `AuthorityGrant` — four ways to describe *obligation*, and **no concept for beneficiary**. "The consultant as first beneficiary" is currently inexpressible in the ratified vocabulary. Fixing it does not require reopening the ontology; it requires every Required Product Behavior to name who benefits.

## 7. The sentence to hold onto

You do not need a new theory. The lever is already ratified at the top of the outcome hierarchy, and the light/heavy reuse split, the beneficiary question and the expert-judgement primitive all exist in the corpus as sentences that were written once and never promoted.

**What is missing is not definition. It is one arc in which a named person is measurably better off — and a condition under which you would admit they are not.**
