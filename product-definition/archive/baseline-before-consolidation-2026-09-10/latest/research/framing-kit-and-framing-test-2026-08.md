# Consultry — Framing Kit and Framing Test

**Status:** Generative, not critical. Candidate language, none ratified. Built to be tested in front of people, not adopted from the page.
**Date:** 2026-08-07
**Purpose:** Convert the moat, the journeys and the expertise lever into language a managing partner reacts to — and use that same act to get the market evidence the corpus lacks.

---

## 1. One correction before the framing

You wrote: *"the problems we want to solve are rather well defined."*

**They are well *specified*. Your own register says they are not well *validated*, and the gap between those two words is where framing goes wrong.**

From the Priority-Problem Evidence & Assumption Register:

> *"Existing research does **not** establish the materiality or frequency of Consultry's broad Blind-Spot Coverage, the proposed archetype-specific buying moments, willingness to pay, recurring retention, cross-project compounding, or Consultry causality."*

> Feature-Pain Map conflict: it *"labels pain inventory `belegt`"* — actual status: *"Evidence quality varies from association studies to vendor surveys and qualitative internal theses."*

> Central claim — *collective expertise becomes actionable* — **"Medium for problem; low for mechanism."** That is your **highest**-confidence claim. Every buying-moment claim is rated **Low**.

Note this is the coherence-over-consequence trap wearing a new costume. *"Well defined"* is a coherence property. It is satisfied by a good taxonomy. It says nothing about which pain bites hard enough to open a wallet.

**Why this matters for framing specifically:** Agently's copy works because it is aimed at a pain their ICP confirmed by paying. Framing is downstream of knowing which pain bites. **Reframe an unvalidated problem and you get better marketing for a possibly-wrong problem** — and because good framing *feels* like traction, you'll believe it longer.

**The resolution, and it is the useful part of this document: framing work and validation work are the same activity if you do the framing in front of people rather than on the page.** Section 6. Everything before it is ammunition for that.

## 2. What "focus on the moat" should and should not mean

Three different things get called moat. Only one of them is framing work.

**(a) Moat as investor argument.** Corpus compounding → switching cost. Legitimate, already written, currently at zero because you have no corpora. Keep it in the deck. **It does not belong in customer framing at all.**

> A moat you have to explain is not a moat. It is a hope with a diagram.

Note what Agently does: they argue moat *nowhere* in their marketing. Their entire customer-facing surface argues **value**. Moat is a conversation between founders and investors; a managing partner has never once chosen a vendor because the vendor had good switching costs.

**(b) Moat as product architecture.** This is the real work, and it is the reuse arc from the previous review: move compounding from an optional closing scene to the opening input. Firm knowledge arrives on minute three, visibly, from a named prior project. That is not framing — that is the single highest-value change to the prototype, and it makes the moat *felt* rather than argued.

**(c) Moat as something that must start compounding.** Corpus compounding requires corpora. Every month without customers is a month the moat does not start, and compounding curves punish late starts disproportionately. **This is an argument for going outside, not for defining the moat better.**

So: yes to (b) with real urgency, yes to (a) in the deck only, and (c) is the reason not to spend another quarter on either.

## 3. What "focus on the journeys" should mean

Not polishing the three you have. **Building the one that does not exist.**

The three ratified threads are exception-triggered: a signal, a contradiction, a conflict. Across ten co-work moments, not one terminates in *"the consultant now has a thing they'd otherwise have spent two hours making."* There is no scene in seven documents where a named person's afternoon gets measurably better.

The missing journey — and it is the one that carries your expertise lever, your framing, and your moat simultaneously:

> **A consultant, mid-work, receives firm knowledge they did not know existed — a prior decision from another mandate, with who made it, why, on what basis, and what does not transfer — and their work gets visibly better in the next twenty minutes. Then their own judgement becomes available to the next person.**

That single arc is: the CONNECT verb, the USE verb, the REUSE verb, light reuse (no rights review needed), the senior-bottleneck answer, the moat made felt, and the only scene in your corpus a partner would repeat to a colleague.

**Build that one. It is worth more than the other three combined**, because it is the only one a horizontal company brain structurally cannot copy — a startup's brain has no other mandate to draw the precedent from.

## 4. What to take from Agently's framing — and what to leave

Take the **mechanics**. Leave the **register**.

| **The oppositional pair** | *"Shipped, not chatted."* Two words, one contrast, a whole positioning. |

| **The receipt** | An everyday word doing the work of "audit trail". |
| **Self-limiting honesty** | *"Agently isn't trying to be Glean."* Buyer-reported figures footnoted as such. Identical to your † discipline — it reads as credibility, not weakness. |
| **The stale-document line** | *"A stale doc doesn't quietly override current reality."* You have better machinery and no sentence. |

| Leave | Why |
|---|---|
| **The swagger** | *"Run a 500-person company with five"* / *"the autonomous company isn't a prediction, it's a deadline."* DACH professional-services buyers read US-startup maximalism as unseriousness. Your own design principle is professional restraint — apply it to copy. |
| **Labour substitution** | *"AI employees"* prices your audience's product at zero. Structurally unavailable to you and you should be glad. |

**The register you want:** concrete, specific, restrained, slightly understated. A German managing partner trusts precision, not energy. Agently's structure at half the volume.

## 5. The framing candidates

None ratified. All written to be tested. German is primary (customer-facing); English follows for deck and investor use.

### The core line

> **„Ihre Erfahrung. In jedem Mandat."**
> *Your firm's judgement. In every mandate.*

Short, active, memorable, and it names the thing only you can claim. Everything below is a variation or a support for it.

---

### 5.1 The senior bottleneck — Boutique, strongest candidate

> **„Ihre beste Beratung steckt in drei Kalendern."**
> *Your best consulting is stuck in three calendars.*
>
> Sub: „Consultry macht die Urteile Ihrer erfahrensten Leute verfügbar — auch dann, wenn diese es nicht sind."
> *Consultry makes your most experienced people's judgement available — including when they are not.*

Why it works: names a person-shaped pain a 15-person partner feels weekly, converts your ratified promise (*"die kleine Senior-Capacity kann mehr und bessere Arbeit bewältigen"*) into something visceral, and it is the one claim no horizontal tool can make.

### 5.2 Knowledge as inventory

> **„Sie verkaufen Wissen, das Sie nicht wiederfinden."**
> *You sell knowledge you can't find again.*
>
> Alt, blunter: **„Ihr Wissen ist kein Overhead. Es ist Ihr Produkt."**
> *Your knowledge isn't overhead. It's your product.*

Why it works: this is the asymmetry that defines your category against every horizontal brain, in one sentence a partner immediately agrees with.

### 5.3 The superseded basis — the ERP scene

> **„Was Sie letzte Woche freigegeben haben, gilt heute vielleicht nicht mehr."**
> *What you signed off last week may not hold today.*
>
> Sub: „Consultry zeigt, wenn sich die Grundlage einer Aussage geändert hat — bevor der Kunde es tut."
> *Consultry shows you when the basis for a claim has changed — before your client does.*

Why it works: it is your most dramatised, best-fixtured scene, and *"before your client does"* is the consequence that makes it a purchase rather than a nicety.

### 5.4 Duplicate work

> **„Das haben wir vor anderthalb Jahren schon gelöst. Nur wusste es niemand."**
> *We solved this eighteen months ago. Nobody knew.*
>
> Sub: „Consultry erkennt, wo parallel am selben Problem gearbeitet wird — mit Quelle, Kontext und dem, was nicht übertragbar ist."

Why it works: it is a quotation, not a claim. Partners hear themselves. And the closing clause — *what does not transfer* — is the credibility move that separates you from a search box.

### 5.5 Against the horizontal company brain

> **„Andere Systeme wissen, was in Ihrer Firma passiert. Consultry weiß, was Ihre Firma kann — und wer dafür geradesteht."**
> *A company brain knows what your company is doing. Consultry knows what your firm can do — and who answers for it.*

Deck/English variant: **"A company brain is built so a company needs less of what it knows. A consultancy sells what it knows."**

### 5.6 The anti-substitution frame

> **„Consultry ersetzt keine Berater. Es lässt die Erfahrung Ihrer Berater in jedem Mandat mitarbeiten."**
> *Consultry doesn't replace consultants. It puts your consultants' experience to work in every mandate.*

Why it matters: pre-empts the day-rate objection, aligns with your ratified seat-paradox answer (*Wachstum, nicht Schrumpfung*), and takes the lane the entire "AI employees" category has vacated.

### 5.7 The ceiling line — your version of *"everyone else stops at the answer"*

> **„Eine Antwort ist noch keine Beratung."**
> *An answer isn't advice yet.*
>
> Sub: „Beratung heißt: jemand steht dafür gerade. Consultry macht sichtbar, worauf sich das stützt."
> *Advice means someone stands behind it. Consultry makes visible what that rests on.*

This is the strongest structural line in the set. It works against ChatGPT, against Glean, against a horizontal brain, and against a proposal generator, in five words — and it is true in a way theirs isn't.

## 6. The framing test — this is the actual recommendation

Do not adopt any line above. **Run them.** The instrument doubles as the market evidence your register says you lack.

**Setup.** Twelve DACH partners — boutique and growing-specialist, mixed. Thirty minutes each. No demo in the first half.

**Part 1 — pain, unprompted (10 min).** Three questions, no others:
1. What did you lose in the last twelve months, and why?
2. Who is your bottleneck person, and what happens when they're unavailable?
3. What do you already pay for that touches any of this?

Record verbatim. **Their nouns are your copy.** You are collecting language, not validating hypotheses.

**Part 2 — the framing card sort (10 min).** Print §5.1–5.7 on seven cards, no branding, no product name.
- *"Which of these is you? Rank the top three."*
- *"Which one is not a problem at all?"* ← the most valuable answer in the whole session
- *"If one of these disappeared tomorrow, what would that be worth?"*

**Part 3 — the scene, then the only question that matters (10 min).** Show one scene, not a tour. Then: *"What would you pay to have this in September, and what would you need to see first?"*

**What you get from one motion:**

| Output | Which part |
|---|---|
| Which pain actually bites (currently rated **Low** across the board) | 1 + 2 |
| Buying-moment evidence per archetype | 1 + 3 |
| Their language, for copy that sounds like them and not like you | 1 |
| Which framing lands — and which is dead | 2 |
| Willingness-to-pay signal | 3 |
| Design partners | all of it |

**The falsification condition, since you now have a reason to write one:** if fewer than half rank the same card in their top three, you do not have a framing problem. **You have a segmentation problem** — the pain differs by archetype more than the model assumes — and the response is to narrow the ICP, not to rewrite the copy.

## 7. Sequence

1. **Pick the scene** — ERP Wave-1 / Reconciliation v3. It is your best-fixtured, most visceral asset.
2. **Write the firm-knowledge fixture.** One named method, one named proof, one named prior decision with author and rationale, one `Project Nord` overlap with a stated non-transferable part. Writing, not engineering. Half a day. **Without this every line in §5 is a promise you cannot show.**
3. **Ten screens, one week.** Not the 62-item WBS.
4. **Print the cards.** Half an hour.
5. **Book twelve conversations.** This is the only step that has ever been the bottleneck.
6. **Rewrite §5 from their words.** The version you come back with will be better than this one, because it will contain sentences you did not invent.

---

*Framing is not a writing task you complete and then take to market. It is a market task whose output happens to be writing. The lines above are hypotheses with good grammar — worth exactly as much as the twelve conversations that test them.*
