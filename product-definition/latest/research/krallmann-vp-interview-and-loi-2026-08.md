# Krallmann VP — Interview Guide and LOI Skeleton

**Status:** Operational. Use in the room, not in the repo.
**Date:** 2026-08-07
**Context:** Interview and possible LOI with a VP at Krallmann Consulting. Caspar Hertel is the warm path.

---

## 0. The frame — read this before anything else

> **Let the LOI choose the slice.**

You have spent months trying to select the first slice analytically — coverage views, journey families, reference threads, depth classes. A VP at a real consultancy naming the job they'd pay to have solved settles that question in forty-five minutes, with authority no taxonomy can give you.

So the goal of this meeting is **not** to validate the product. It is to get one sentence, in their words, describing a job — and then to get that sentence into a document with a date and a number next to it.

**Two failure modes, both likely, both avoidable:**

1. **You demo, they're polite, you get a warm LOI about "exploring a partnership."** That document is worth nothing to you, to them, or to an investor. It happens because a warm contact will always say yes to something vague.
2. **You get a real LOI for a job that only Krallmann has.** Then you have consulting revenue, which is fine — but do not mistake it for product. The test is in §4.

---

## 1. Before you go in

**Decide which conversation this is.** It changes everything downstream:

- **VP is the economic buyer** — can commit budget and a team. Then the LOI can carry a pilot fee and a date, and you should ask for both.
- **VP is a champion** — has to sell it internally. Then the job of the meeting is to arm them: they need a one-pager they can forward, a number they can defend, and a named internal blocker you help them plan around. Ask directly: *"Wenn Sie das intern vertreten müssten — wen müssten Sie überzeugen, und woran scheitert es normalerweise?"*

**Bring one scene, not a tour.** The ERP Wave-1 / Reconciliation v3 case is Krallmann-shaped and it is your best-fixtured asset. Show that, and nothing else. If they ask what else it does, say *"das zeige ich Ihnen beim nächsten Mal — ich will erst verstehen, ob das hier überhaupt Ihr Problem ist."* That answer builds more credibility than a feature list.

**Write the firm-knowledge fixture first.** One named method, one named prior decision with its author and rationale, one overlap from a second mandate with a stated non-transferable part. Half a day of writing. Without it the scene is a promise; with it, it is a demonstration.

## 2. The interview — 45 minutes

**Open (3 min).** *"Ich will heute nichts verkaufen. Ich will verstehen, wie bei Ihnen tatsächlich gearbeitet wird — und am Ende zeige ich Ihnen genau eine Sache."*

That sentence buys you the whole first half. Use it.

### Part 1 — the job (20 min). Past behaviour only, never hypotheticals.

This is the discipline that matters most with a warm contact. *"Wäre das nützlich?"* always gets a yes and tells you nothing. Ask about things that already happened:

1. **„Erzählen Sie mir vom letzten Mandat, das Sie verloren haben — oder fast. Woran lag es wirklich?"**
2. **„Wenn jemand wissen muss, ob die Firma so etwas schon einmal gemacht hat — was passiert dann konkret? Wen fragt er?"**
3. **„Wer ist bei Ihnen die Person, die alle brauchen und niemand bekommt? Was passiert, wenn sie nicht verfügbar ist?"**
4. **„Wann wurde zuletzt etwas doppelt gemacht, weil niemand wusste, dass es das schon gab?"**
5. **„Was zahlen Sie heute schon für Werkzeuge, die irgendetwas davon berühren?"** ← the only real willingness-to-pay signal you will get before a signature
6. **„Wenn sich daran nichts ändert — was kostet Sie das im Jahr?"** ← push for a number, even a rough one. Their number, not yours.

**Write down their nouns.** Not your interpretation — their actual words. That vocabulary is your copy, your slice name, and the first line of the LOI.

### Part 2 — one scene (10 min)

Show it. Say almost nothing. The two things to watch for:

- Do they interrupt? An interruption is the strongest signal in the room.
- Do they start telling *you* a story from their own firm? That means it landed.

Then one question only: **„Wo ist das bei Ihnen anders?"** Their corrections are more valuable than their approval.

### Part 3 — the commitment conversation (12 min)

In this order. Do not skip ahead.

7. **„Was müsste wahr sein, damit Krallmann das im Q4 an einem echten Mandat einsetzt?"**
8. **„Was würde es verhindern?"** ← the most valuable question in the meeting. Ask it, then stay quiet.
9. **„Wer muss noch Ja sagen?"**
10. **„Wenn wir das in acht Wochen an einem echten Fall zeigen — würden Sie das mit einem Piloten begleiten?"**

Only if 10 is a yes: **„Was wäre Ihnen das wert?"** Let them name the number first. If they name one at all, you have willingness to pay — the single claim your evidence register says cannot be inferred.

## 3. The LOI — what makes one worth having

A letter that says *"Krallmann Consulting bekundet Interesse an einer Evaluierung"* is worth nothing. Six elements make it worth something to you, to them, and in a raise:

| Element | Why |
|---|---|
| **The job, in their words** | This is your first slice, chosen by a customer instead of a taxonomy. Quote them verbatim. |
| **Named scope** | Which team, how many people, which mandate or corpus. Vagueness here is how pilots die. |
| **What Krallmann provides** | Documents, named contact, a stated number of hours. A pilot with no customer input is a demo. |
| **The success condition — theirs** | Written by them, testable, in one sentence. **This is your falsification condition, delivered by a customer.** You have been unable to write one for months; they can write it in a minute. |
| **A pilot fee** | Even €1.500/month. The point is not revenue. A signature next to a number is the only willingness-to-pay evidence that exists. Free pilots validate curiosity. |
| **Reference permission** | Ask. Often granted, and it is worth more than the fee. |

**Binding on participation, non-binding on purchase.** That distinction is what makes it signable quickly, and you should say so out loud — it lowers the bar and it is honest.

### Skeleton — `Unverbindliche Absichtserklärung`

> **Zwischen** Krallmann Consulting **und** Consultry
>
> **1. Ausgangslage.** *[Ihr Satz, wörtlich — der Job, den sie beschrieben haben.]*
>
> **2. Gegenstand des Piloten.** Consultry stellt bis zum *[Datum]* eine Arbeitsfassung bereit, die genau diesen Fall abdeckt: *[der eine Slice, konkret, keine Feature-Liste]*.
>
> **3. Umfang.** *[N]* Nutzer aus *[Team]*, angewendet auf *[Mandat / Korpus]*. Laufzeit *[8 Wochen]*.
>
> **4. Beitrag Krallmann.** Bereitstellung von *[Unterlagen / Projektkontext]*; benannte Ansprechperson *[Name]*; ca. *[X]* Stunden Feedback über die Laufzeit.
>
> **5. Erfolgskriterium.** *[Ein Satz, von Krallmann formuliert. Prüfbar. Nicht von Consultry vorgeschlagen.]*
>
> **6. Kommerzielles.** Pilotgebühr *[€]* pro Monat. Bei Erreichen des Erfolgskriteriums besteht die Absicht, über eine reguläre Nutzung zu sprechen; eine Kaufverpflichtung entsteht nicht.
>
> **7. Daten und Vertraulichkeit.** Mandantendaten verbleiben in einer isolierten Umgebung; keine Verwendung für Modelltraining; keine automatischen externen Wirkungen — Ergebnisse werden ausschließlich von Krallmann-Mitarbeitenden weiterverwendet.
>
> **8. Referenz.** Krallmann stimmt einer Nennung als Design-Partner zu *[ja / nach Abschluss / nein]*.
>
> **9. Charakter.** Diese Erklärung bindet hinsichtlich der Teilnahme, nicht hinsichtlich eines Kaufs.

Clause 7 is worth noticing: it is your §3 scope restrictions from the strictness dial, written as a customer reassurance. **The thing that makes v1 cheap to build is the same thing that makes it easy to sign.** That is not a coincidence — it is the argument for the dial.

## 4. The generalisation test — run it within a week

One design partner's job becomes the product only if it is not only theirs. The check is cheap:

> Take the exact sentence the VP gave you in §2. Send it to Julian at SECUINFRA and ask: **„Ist das bei euch auch so — und wie oft?"**

- **Yes, and it happens regularly** → that is your first slice, and now you have two firms behind it.
- **Yes, but rarely** → real, but not a wedge. Keep it, do not lead with it.
- **No** → it is a Krallmann job. Build it if you want the revenue, but call it consulting and do not let it set the roadmap.

You are unusually well set up for this test — two consultancies, two warm contacts, one question. Most founders would pay a lot for that.

## 6. Afterwards, within 24 hours

Write down, before the memory decays:

1. Their exact words for the job.
2. Their number for what it costs them today.
3. What they said would stop it.
4. Who else has to say yes.
5. Whether they interrupted the scene, and where.

Then put items 1–4 into the Evidence Register as **primary evidence**, not as a side note. Several claims currently rated **Low** will move on the strength of one conversation — and that register entry is the first line of your next investor conversation.
