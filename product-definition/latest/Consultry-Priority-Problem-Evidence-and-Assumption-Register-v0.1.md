# Consultry — Priority-Problem Evidence & Assumption Register v0.1

**Status:** Research baseline for Product/Business-Domain Wayfinder; not customer validation  
**Date:** 02.08.2026  
**Scope:** Ratified Target-Consultancy outcome, two first-class operating archetypes, Knowledge-to-Action, Blind-Spot Coverage, Work Effectiveness & Efficiency, Synergy & Reuse, value cadence and Core Value Proof  
**Decision source:** [Ratify the product problem and outcome hierarchy](./wayfinder/consultry-product-platform-baseline/tickets/ratify-the-product-problem-and-outcome-hierarchy.md)

---

## 1. Executive verdict

The ratified hierarchy is a coherent **Product Direction**, not an empirically validated product effect.

- Current primary research supports that AI is already material in consulting, especially in knowledge/research, proposals and customer-project work; consulting firms face efficiency, differentiation, capacity and rate-model pressure.
- Knowledge-work and consultant experiments support the potential for meaningful speed/quality gains, but also show task-dependent failure and performance degradation. They support bounded, contextual Human-AI collaboration and validation, not autonomous or universally beneficial AI.
- Existing research does **not** establish the materiality or frequency of Consultry's broad Blind-Spot Coverage, the proposed archetype-specific buying moments, willingness to pay, recurring retention, cross-project compounding, or Consultry causality.
- The Partner-led Specialist Boutique is strategically first-class but under-researched in the current artifact stack. Older Product Vision/MVP documents explicitly excluded parts of this segment.
- Current primary evidence is strongest for Germany and includes a broad Austrian Information & Consulting sample; no current Swiss consulting-specific primary source was found for these claims. “DACH evidence” would therefore overstate the geographic coverage.
- Founder experience and Dogfood #0 are valuable first-hand Domain-/Problem-Evidence once concrete cases are captured; existing Personas, pitch narratives and internally designed acceptance scenarios remain scenario evidence or hypotheses. None of these alone is independent market validation or direct Consultry product-effect evidence.
- The current measurement stack proves at most an `Opportunity-to-Concept` artifact milestone. It does not yet prove `Opportunity-to-Project`, Project/Delivery, Management/Operations, Retention or Compounding outcomes.

No percentage or economic effect in this register may be turned into a Consultry claim unless the cited study population, task, baseline and limitations travel with it.

## 2. Evidence vocabulary

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **S — Supported problem signal** | A credible source directly observes the underlying problem or behavior in a relevant population. It does not prove Consultry's effect. |
| **I — Indirect signal** | Credible evidence exists, but for another population, task, geography or firm type. |
| **H — Product hypothesis** | Internally coherent and worth testing, but not currently established by external or first-hand customer evidence. |
| **U — Unvalidated outcome claim** | A claim about causality, buying behavior, retention, economics or downstream effect that requires interviews or a product experiment. |
| **D — Ratified domain/product decision** | A deliberate definition of what Consultry is or should cover. It is not empirical evidence that the market values or the product achieves it. |

Evidence is attached to the smallest claim possible. A supported problem must never be silently promoted into a supported solution, product effect or purchase priority.

## 6. Current-workaround hypotheses to validate

The following patterns are present in internal artifacts but are not yet documented as observed customer baselines:

- ask known colleagues directly or through Slack/Teams/chat;
- search shared drives, DMS, Confluence, email and previous project folders;
- recreate knowledge, reports, methods, concepts or proposal content when the right artifact or expert is not found;
- keep expertise/skills in CVs, presentation decks, spreadsheets and people's memory;
- coordinate opportunities and decisions in Excel, email, meetings and personal memory;
- continue proposal/concept work in Word after manual copy/export;
- clarify project and management questions by phone or recurring status meeting and maintain parallel local trackers;
- manually reconcile CRM, PSA, PM, DMS, collaboration and finance systems;
- rely on a Partner/Principal as the human integration point across Sales, Delivery, Knowledge and Operations;
- defer cross-project reuse until an individual remembers a similar prior case.

For each workaround, Discovery must capture the last real occurrence, trigger, actors, systems, elapsed time, failure/quality consequence, commercial consequence, frequency, authority constraints and what was tried already.

## 7. Required Design-Partner interviews

Use non-leading **last-time** questions. Do not ask whether the respondent “likes” the Consultry concept before establishing current behavior.

### 7.1 Common core

1. “Tell me about the last important opportunity, project task, concept, deliverable or management decision where the required firm knowledge or expert was not immediately available.”
2. “What triggered the work, who noticed it, and who was accountable for the result?”
3. “Walk me through every system, file, meeting and person used from trigger to decision or deliverable.”
4. “Where did information conflict, remain uncertain or arrive too late? What consequence actually occurred?”
5. “What did you recreate even though something similar may have existed?”
6. “Which parts required judgment or authority that you would never delegate to an AI workflow?”
7. “Who would fund a solution, who would champion adoption, who could veto it, and from which budget?”
8. “What observable result would justify continuing after a pilot—and what result would make you stop?”

### 7.2 Partner-led Specialist Boutique

1. “Tell me about the last time a Partner or Principal had to cover a Sales, Bid, Knowledge, Project-Control, Operations or Management responsibility because no specialist function existed.”
2. “What higher-value work was delayed or dropped, and was the limitation capacity, missing expertise, fragmented context or authority?”
3. “Which responsibility is repeatedly compressed onto the same people, and how often does that create rework, delay or missed risk?”
4. “Would the firm pay to support this job before hiring a specialist function? What existing spend or pain would fund it?”
5. “For firms around 5–14 people: is the pain strong enough, frequent enough and budgeted enough to contradict the older Anti-ICP assumption?”

### 7.3 Growing Specialist Consultancy

1. “Tell me about the last material issue that crossed a team, practice or system boundary and was discovered late.”
2. “Which handoff lost context, and which role owned recovery?”
3. “When was similar work performed twice because teams did not know about one another?”
4. “Who owns the cross-functional outcome when Sales, Delivery, Staffing, Knowledge, Commercials and Finance disagree?”
5. “Would the operating champion be Delivery, Operations, Knowledge/Transformation, a Partner or another responsibility—and who controls the budget?”

Minimum evidence target before treating archetype-specific buying moments as grounded: several concrete last-time cases in each archetype, covering more than one job family and including at least one rejected or low-pain case. A numeric sample size is deliberately not invented here; the research plan should set it based on saturation and decision risk.

## 8. Product experiments

### E1 — Core Value Proof against current workaround

- Select one material real case and freeze the baseline input/context.
- Observe the current workaround first or reconstruct it with artifacts and timestamps.
- Run the same job through the configured Consultry flow.
- Capture source coverage, unresolved material issues, accepted/edited/rejected output, restart behavior, elapsed work, handoffs and resulting Decision/Action/state change.
- Pass/fail is scenario-specific but must demonstrate human-dispositioned progress, not merely generated content.

### E2 — Blind-Spot Finding usefulness

- Use historical cases with known missed issues plus live cases where ground truth is incomplete.
- Have responsible domain reviewers classify finding correctness, materiality, novelty, timing and actionability.
- Track false-positive review cost and rejected findings as first-class evidence.
- Do not claim recall until a defensible labeled universe exists.

### E3 — Knowledge-to-Action comparison

- Compare the relevant current route—search, ask colleague, generic AI, template or prior project—with the Consultry route.
- Measure whether the user can identify sources, understand uncertainty and take or reject a responsible next action.
- Separate faster retrieval from better decision/work quality.

### E4 — Repeated-value/retention test

- Observe several naturally recurring jobs rather than prescribing logins.
- Record which roles return, for which outcomes, and whether use displaces or merely adds coordination work.
- Treat weekly active seats as telemetry, not proof of retained value by itself.

### E5 — Governed Reuse Application

- Start from a real cross-project similarity proposed by the system or a person.
- Validate the Pattern, Source Lineage, applicability, exclusions and rights/confidentiality state.
- Observe an actual Reuse Application and its adaptation, not just a recommendation.
- Measure Delivery/Quality effect against a stated baseline; only then create an economic Value Case.

### E6 — Opportunity-to-Project extension

- Extend the current Concept measurement through external/internal commitment, ClientContract/SOW as applicable, project creation and responsible handoff.
- Verify that Consultry contributed to the transition without asserting win-rate causality from a single case.

## 9. Consequences for Product Definition and Pitch

1. Keep the ratified outcome hierarchy, but label Product causality and economic outcomes as hypotheses until experiments provide evidence.
2. Use current BDU evidence to support the **market context**: AI is already mainstream in consulting, knowledge/research and proposals are common use areas, and human communication/coordination remains central.
3. Use the consultant experiment to justify bounded Human-AI workflows, task/context specificity and Validation—not a universal “AI makes consultants 40% better” claim.
4. Do not present a broad AI day-rate collapse. Current BDU evidence says most firms had not yet seen a material AI effect on fee calculation, although many expect future change.
5. Do not use Bitkom's 109,000 unfilled IT roles as evidence that a specific boutique lacks Bid, Knowledge or Operations functions.
6. Do not use RFP vendor benchmarks as DACH-consultancy PMF evidence. They can motivate Discovery and a Concept reference scenario.
7. Keep Blind-Spot Coverage broad as a Product Domain decision, but test and report it by concrete context, consequence, timing and reviewer disposition.
8. Keep Compounding in the full Product definition, but pitch it as a governed loop to validate until real Reuse Applications and Value Cases exist.
9. Reconcile stale ICP, path, Buyer, proof and measurement statements only through the later canonical-artifact reconciliation ticket; do not silently rewrite technical/build commitments inside this research ticket.

## 10. Research closure criterion

This register resolves what can be established from existing artifacts and current external research. It does **not** close the empirical gaps. Those become explicit Product Discovery and experiment obligations consumed by Actor/JTBD, Journey, Acceptance and Horizon decisions.
