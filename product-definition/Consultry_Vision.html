01 - Executive thesis

Consultry is not another chat interface. It is governed AI work infrastructure for selling and managing consulting work.

Phase 1 should not try to replace the tools hard technical consultants already love for coding, technical troubleshooting, or complex implementation work. The first wedge is the commercial and management layer: opportunities, team fit, proposal quality, SOW drafting, commercial assumptions, approval, export, and compliance.

Professional-grade consulting outputs from governed internal context, without forcing users to rebuild prompt context manually every time.

Long-term ambition

An agentic operating system for consultancy workflows: the place where client context, project history, capability, commercial logic, and governance meet.

Phase 1 wedge

A governed opportunity and proposal workspace for 10-30 person DACH IT/digital consulting boutiques.

What it replaces first

Excel staffing boards, Word proposal assembly, SharePoint knowledge search, and cross-tool context switching.

What it does not replace first

Codex, Claude Code, IDEs, GitHub, Jira, deep technical execution tools, full PSA, ERP, payroll, or autonomous legal advice.

Core product definition

Consultry turns a market/client signal or RFP into an opportunity brief, comparable-project evidence, team proposal, tailored CVs, proposal draft, SOW/commercial draft, and export-ready review package - with source traceability, access control, brand/methodology rules, and approval built into the workflow.

02 - Why now

The market wants AI leverage, but DACH consulting cannot afford unmanaged AI work.

The opportunity is created by four forces moving together: IT consulting demand, existing-client economics, generative AI adoption, and rising governance expectations around worker data, source claims, and data sovereignty.

49.0B EUR German consulting revenue 2025

BDU reported 49.0B EUR market revenue in 2025 and expects 51.1B EUR in 2026.

+4.7% IT consulting growth forecast 2026

BDU identifies IT consulting as one of the dynamic 2026 growth drivers.

80% Average revenue from existing clients

BDU's 2021 benchmark note cites 2019 data: 80% overall, with smaller consultancies around 62%.

2026+ AI governance pressure

AI Act, GDPR, BetrVG, and customer sovereignty concerns make unmanaged AI workflows harder to defend.

Market implication

The product should not promise generic productivity. It should promise professional-grade, governed consulting work products in a category where context quality, claim accuracy, brand consistency, and permission boundaries directly affect revenue and trust.

03 - Beachhead customer

Phase 1 starts with 10-30 person DACH IT and digital consulting boutiques.

Earlier documents explored 30-200 person consultancies. The refined Phase 1 wedge is smaller by design: fewer procurement layers, faster founder decisions, tighter methodology, higher tool-switching pain, and a stronger need to make every partner and consultant operate above their baseline through governed AI workflows.

Ideal customer profile

  • 10-30 consultants.
  • DACH-first IT, digital, cloud, SAP, data/AI, automation, or transformation boutique.
  • High project-history value and recurring client relationships.
  • Founder/managing partner still close to sales, staffing, delivery, and commercial decisions.

Buyer and sponsor

  • Founder or managing partner owns budget and risk.
  • Project lead or BD/project lead hybrid owns daily opportunity execution.
  • Operations/backoffice owns templates, exports, data hygiene, and access rules.

Consultant role in Phase 1

  • Validates consultant profile claims and tailored CV facts.
  • Contributes reusable project knowledge.
  • Provides availability and technical review when needed.
  • Does not have to abandon Codex, Claude Code, GitHub, Jira, or IDE workflows.

30-100 person consultancy Expansion Higher ACV, but more stakeholder/process complexity. Use after boutique proof.

04 - Problem statement

Consultancies lose quality and trust when high-value work is assembled from scattered context.

Proposal and staffing work is not blocked by lack of AI access. It is blocked by repeated context packaging, manual source selection, inconsistent prompt craft, weak permission boundaries, and fragile export workflows.

Excel staffing board

Availability, skills, project roles, utilization, and delivery risk sit in manually maintained spreadsheets that drift from reality.

Word proposal flow

Strong proposals require reassembling context, CVs, references, pricing, assumptions, and tone from scratch.

SharePoint knowledge search

Project history exists, but users still need to know what to search for, what is current, and what may be reused.

Teams and Outlook context

Calendar availability and meeting context matter, but silent mailbox mining creates compliance and trust risk.

Generic AI tools

They can draft, but users must repeatedly paste context, tone, brand, source, and methodology rules - often outside the allowed data boundary.

PSA and CRM tools

They manage projects, sales, and finance, but do not natively transform project history into governed proposal and team intelligence.

Critical insight

The comparison is not "Consultry is faster than ChatGPT." A user can do parts of this manually with ChatGPT and SharePoint. The question is whether they can do it safely, source-bound, brand-accurately, repeatedly, with the right permissions, and with low hallucination risk.

05 - Phase 1 product scope

Phase 1 is the commercial operating layer, not the full consultancy execution stack.

The product must feel like an integrated operating layer, but it should not overbuild PSA, ERP, HR, or coding capabilities before the commercial wedge is proven.

In scope for Phase 1

  • Opportunity Workspace as the main work surface.
  • Market/client signal and client request/RFP as two entry paths.
  • Project-history graph and graph-RAG over approved sources.
  • Team proposal, capacity view, free/busy availability, and aggregated utilization/forecast context.
  • Tailored CVs with source-bound consultant claims and claim review.
  • Proposal draft and SOW/commercial draft from approved templates and rules.
  • Brand, methodology, client, opportunity, and artifact rule layers.
  • RBAC, source access gating, approval gates, audit trail, and export controls.
  • DOCX, PPTX, and PDF export from a canonical Consultry artifact model.
  • Microsoft 365 integration for calendar free/busy, metadata, and user-selected meeting notes.

Explicit non-goals for Phase 1

  • No coding agent or replacement for Codex, Claude Code, IDEs, Jira, or GitHub.
  • No autonomous legal advisor or legal compliance guarantee.
  • No full PSA replacement, full time tracking, payroll, tax, or ERP.
  • No silent employee monitoring or hidden performance scoring.
  • No fully automated broad market-intelligence or outbound automation platform.
  • No autonomous external sending, filing, signing, or submission.
  • No unmanaged prompt marketplace or uncontrolled agent runtime.

Scope discipline

Delivery and finance matter because they affect proposal credibility, margin, utilization, and forecast. They should enter Phase 1 as commercial intelligence and assumptions, not as full delivery management, billing, or accounting replacement.

06 - Core workflow

The Opportunity Workspace is the first product surface.

The workspace unifies guided AI workflows, graph retrieval, team logic, proposal drafting, commercial reasoning, approvals, and export. It adapts by user and scenario: cockpit for the partner, canvas for proposal work, cards for approvals, tables for backoffice, and claim review for consultants.

1 Signal or RFP Market/client signal, pasted source, user-selected note, or formal request.
2 Context capture Guided intake resolves client, need, scope, constraints, rules, and allowed sources.
3 Comparable projects Graph-RAG finds relevant project history, methods, artifacts, and evidence.
4 Team proposal Capacity, skills, project roles, and availability produce explainable team options.
5 Claim review Consultant facts are source-bound, contestable, and approved before reuse.
6 CVs and proposal Tailored CVs and proposal narrative follow brand, method, and source rules.
7 SOW and commercials Drafts include deliverables, assumptions, exclusions, rate logic, and margin view.
8 Approval Project lead, managing partner, and consultant checks are logged and auditable.
9 Export DOCX, PPTX, and PDF generated from the canonical artifact model.

Entry path A: client request / RFP

This should be the deterministic demo path. It has clearer input, output, and quality criteria.

Entry path B: market / account signal

Phase 1 should support curated or user-supplied signal inputs. Automated broad monitoring belongs later.

07 - AI product model

The unit of product is a governed AI skill, not a raw prompt.

High-quality AI work in consulting requires repeated context packaging: allowed sources, retrieval strategy, methodology, tone, brand, source evidence, output schema, approval policy, and export constraints. Consultry packages those patterns into reusable AI skills.

Skill contract

A governed AI skill equals: intent + allowed roles + allowed source classes + retrieval strategy + brand and methodology rules + output schema + PII policy + destination policy + approval requirements + audit events.

08 - Governance baseline

Access gating and RBAC are part of the product promise, not backend plumbing.

Consultry will handle sensitive client context, consultant profiles, project history, meeting notes, and commercial assumptions. The baseline promise must be permission-aware work generation.

Non-negotiable rule

If a user cannot access a source directly, the AI must not be able to use that source indirectly. No permission laundering through summaries, embeddings, draft text, or exported artifacts.

Minimum roles

  • Admin
  • Managing Partner
  • Project Lead / BD Lead
  • Consultant
  • Backoffice / Operations
  • External Reviewer as post-MVP option

Minimum scopes

  • Tenant
  • Client / account
  • Project
  • Document / source
  • Reusable-approved knowledge asset
  • Export destination

Audit events

  • Source ingestion and access changes
  • AI skill execution and source bindings
  • Claim approval, edit, rejection
  • Proposal/SOW approval
  • Export generation and destination

09 - Data and integration strategy

The product only works if project history becomes usable, permissioned intelligence.

"Project history" must be treated as a structured onboarding package, not a vague aspiration. The graph is justified only if it improves retrieval, reasoning, team construction, claim traceability, and source discipline.

Minimum viable data package

  • Project list with client, industry, dates, roles, scope, and outcomes.
  • Old proposals and proposal templates.
  • Consultant CVs and structured skill/certification facts.
  • Case studies, references, methods, delivery documents, and reusable artifacts.
  • SOW/contract templates, approved clauses, assumptions, exclusions, and commercial terms.
  • Rate cards, role cost assumptions, availability/free-busy data, and capacity constraints.
  • Access labels and reuse permissions for every source class.

Phase 1 integrations

  • Microsoft 365: free/busy availability, calendar metadata, and user-selected meeting notes.
  • SharePoint / file storage: approved document ingestion and source-bound retrieval.
  • Optional CRM/PSA import: account, project, and opportunity records where available.
  • Export: DOCX, PPTX, PDF through canonical Consultry artifact model.

Graph hypothesis

The graph is a product bet, not database religion. It must prove measurable value in connecting clients, projects, consultants, skills, roles, methods, artifacts, claims, and commercial assumptions. If graph-native workflows do not outperform simpler Postgres + vector approaches, the architecture should retain a fallback path.

10 - Competitive position

Consultry should avoid fighting PSA vendors head-on in Phase 1.

Established PSA and project-business tools already cover resource planning, project management, time tracking, quoting, invoicing, and financial reporting. Consultry wins where those systems and generic AI tools are weak: consultancy-specific context packaging, proposal intelligence, source-bound claims, permission-aware retrieval, and workflow-specific AI skills.

Alternative What it does well Consultry wedge
PSA tools such as Kantata, Scoro, Productive, MOCO Resource planning, project lifecycle, time, budgets, financials, quoting, invoicing. Project-history-to-proposal intelligence, governed AI skills, source-bound team/CV/proposal generation.
CRM tools Pipeline, account records, contact history, sales activity. Consultant capability, methodology, project proof, commercial assumptions, and proposal pack generation.
Microsoft Copilot + SharePoint Horizontal productivity inside Microsoft 365. Consultancy-specific graph, claim governance, approval hierarchy, export model, and role/source-aware AI skills.
ChatGPT / Claude / generic AI Flexible drafting and reasoning when the user supplies context manually. Pre-packaged consulting workflows that already know allowed sources, brand rules, methodology, approval policy, and export format.
Internal custom tools Fit local process, but often fragile and under-governed. Productized governance, reusable AI skill layer, and investor-scale category potential.
11 - Business value and success metrics

The brand promise is quality under control. The operating metrics still measure speed and decision quality.

The external promise should not be a stopwatch challenge against ChatGPT. The internal measurement system should still track whether the workflow reduces friction, improves team decisions, and produces better proposal packs.

1 Time-to-team

From opportunity brief to approved team proposal. Leading indicator for staffing intelligence.

2 Proposal cycle time

From accepted brief to partner-review-ready proposal pack. Measures workflow compression.

3 Utilization

Lagging outcome. Useful after the customer actually runs capacity decisions through Consultry.

4 Forecast accuracy

Lagging outcome. Depends on repeated opportunity, capacity, and commercial usage.

Quality metric Why it matters Suggested pilot measurement
Source coverage Measures whether factual proposal/CV claims are grounded. Share of claims with approved source binding.
First-draft acceptance Measures professional-grade output quality. Partner/project-lead rating per draft section.
Claim correction rate Measures consultant sovereignty and data quality. Claims edited/rejected before external CV export.
Unauthorized source incidents Measures permission-aware AI promise. Target: zero in pilot.
Export rework Measures readiness of DOCX/PPTX/PDF outputs. Manual formatting time after export.
12 - Phasing

Build enough governance to be credible, but do not build the whole OS before the wedge proves pull.

Phase 0 - Foundation

  • Identity, tenant isolation, RBAC, source permissions.
  • Audit trail and approval primitives.
  • Document ingestion, source binding, and graph/RAG foundation.
  • Brand/method/rule inheritance model.

Phase 1 - Commercial layer

  • Opportunity Workspace.
  • Team proposal, tailored CV, proposal, SOW/commercial draft.
  • Microsoft 365 free/busy and user-selected meeting context.
  • DOCX/PPTX/PDF export and partner approval flow.

Phase 2+ - Expansion

  • Automated market intelligence and prospect workflows.
  • Delivery QA/review operators.
  • Billing handoff and deeper PSA integrations.
  • Expansion to 30-100 and later 100-200 consultancies.
13 - Strategic risks and mitigations

The strongest investor story is candid about the risks.

TAM/ACV mismatch

10-30 person boutiques may have urgent pain but limited budget. Mitigation: paid design partners, clear per-seat plus implementation economics, and expansion path to 30-100 after proof.

Scope creep into full PSA/ERP

Delivery, billing, and accounting can swallow the wedge. Mitigation: Phase 1 finance stays commercial-readiness: price, margin, utilization, forecast, assumptions, and export context.

Data quality and onboarding load

Project history is messy. Mitigation: define a minimum viable data package and make ingestion/claim review part of the product, not custom services hidden in the background.

Worker-management compliance

Team proposal and capacity logic can be sensitive. Mitigation: no hidden scoring, aggregated analytics by default, consultant claim review, approval gates, audit, and works-council-ready configuration.

Microsoft/Copilot platform risk

Horizontal AI will improve. Mitigation: win on consultancy graph, governed AI skills, source-bound claims, artifact-specific approval, and proposal/SOW export workflow.

Graph architecture risk

Graph-native reasoning may not justify complexity. Mitigation: treat graph as a hypothesis with measurable workflow gains and maintain fallback architecture.

14 - Open ambiguities and grilling questions

These questions should stay open until product, sales, and architecture prove them.

A convincing stakeholder artifact should not hide unresolved decisions. These are the points that can break the product if they are answered lazily.

Business model

  • What ACV can a 10-30 person boutique actually pay in year one?
  • Is the first sale a subscription, paid pilot, co-build retainer, or services-led implementation?
  • What is the smallest paid pilot that proves stakeholder pull?

Finance depth

  • Does Phase 1 stop at pricing, margin, utilization, and forecast?
  • Or does it require real billing handoff to be credible?
  • Which commercial fields are required for partner approval?

EU/no-US architecture

  • Is strict EU-only processing mandatory for all customers or an enterprise deployment mode?
  • Which model/vendor stack makes the claim true?
  • What can be promised externally before vendor contracts are final?

Market signal v1

  • Is Phase 1 market signal manual, pasted, curated, or monitored?
  • Which sources are allowed before broad market intelligence is built?
  • Can the demo show market signal without committing to automated prospecting?

Contract boundary

  • What does "complete draft" mean without implying legal advice?
  • Which clauses are template-bound and which require external legal review?
  • Who approves SOW/commercial/legal risk before export?

Graph proof

  • Which three workflows prove graph-RAG is materially better than vector search alone?
  • How will retrieval quality be measured in the pilot?
  • What is the fallback if graph complexity does not pay off?
