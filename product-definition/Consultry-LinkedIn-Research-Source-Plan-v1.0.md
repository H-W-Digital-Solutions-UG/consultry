## 2. Source Discipline

### Public Claim Rules

Use three claim levels:

| Claim level | Allowed wording | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Strong research claim | "In this benchmark/paper, method X improved Y by Z." | Specific paper and task context. |
| Practical implementation claim | "In real implementations, orchestration can shift outcomes materially." | Consultry/internal experience plus at least one research analogue. |
| Strategic thesis | "The next useful systems will be won by architecture, not model choice alone." | Framed as point of view, not empirical fact. |

### Important Caveat On 20-40 Percent Performance Claims

The public LinkedIn line should not say, without context, "harnesses improve performance by 20-40%."

Safe version:

> Depending on task, model, and evaluation setup, orchestration and harness design can move measured performance dramatically. In some reported LM-pipeline settings, optimized orchestration beats baseline prompting by more than 20%, and in some cases by much more.

Reason:

- DSPy reports >25% and >65% gains over standard few-shot prompting in its two case settings, and up to 5-46% / 16-40% over expert-created demonstrations depending on model and case.
- ReAct, Reflexion, Tree of Thoughts, and RAFT report large gains in specific tasks, but they are not a generic "harness impact" benchmark.
- Consultry can say the harness is a major performance surface, but should avoid universal numeric claims until its own evals exist.

Primary source:

- DSPy: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03714

## 4. Post Plan 1 - The Harness

### First Article

**Title:**  
**The Road to Truly Intelligent Systems - The Harness: Why the System Around the Model Changes the Result**

### Thesis

A model alone is not a working AI system. The harness around the model determines what context it sees, which tools it can use, how it plans, how its output is checked, and when a human must approve.

### Consultry Angle

Consultry's category is already "AI Work Harness fuer Beratungen." This post should define what that means in plain language:

- A bounded, job-scoped working environment.
- A `HarnessPack` with corpus, memory, tools, connector grants, capability tokens, output schema, and policy.
- No free tenant-wide search.
- No autonomous outbound action.
- Every factual output checked through SourceBinding, approval, and audit.
- Harness evaluation as a separate layer from prompt evaluation.

The applied example:

> A DACH consultancy wants a grounded concept/proposal section from its own documents. The model is only one part. The harness decides which contract, tender criteria, reference assets, skills, templates, and approval gates are available.

### Audience Problem

Most executives still compare AI systems by model name. This post should move them to comparing the full operating environment.

### Research Questions

- What is the measurable effect of orchestration, prompting, planning, tool use, and pipeline optimization?
- Which "harness" elements have research analogues?
- What should a business buyer ask beyond "which model do you use?"
- How do we explain tool use and orchestration without sounding too technical?

## 5. Post Plan 2 - The Skill Graph

### First Article

**Title:**  
**The Road to Truly Intelligent Systems - The Skill Graph: Mapping What an AI System Can Actually Do**

### Thesis

Useful AI systems need a map of capabilities. A Skill Graph connects tasks, tools, knowledge sources, constraints, evidence, approval rules, and outcomes so the system can reason about what it can do safely and where it needs help.

### Consultry Angle

In Consultry, the Skill Graph is a first-class projection of the Second Brain:

- It models evidence-backed capability claims.
- It links skills to certificates, project experience, source spans, role needs, team-shape requirements, and proposal proof assets.
- It explicitly does **not** rank people or score performance.
- It enables anonymous, aggregated TeamShape for bids.
- It supports source-bound capability proof in the Concept Suite.

Plain-language example:

> A consultancy does not just need to know that "cloud security" exists. It needs to know which projects proved it, which certificates support it, which references can be used, which roles are needed, and which claims are safe to put into a proposal.

### Audience Problem

Companies talk about "skills" as a spreadsheet. AI systems need skills as connected, evidence-backed capability structures.

### Research Questions

- What does AI research mean by "agent skills"?
- How do skills differ from prompts, tools, workflows, and memories?
- How do we connect skill representation to validation and evidence?
- How can this be explained without triggering HR/performance-scoring concerns?

## 6. Post Plan 3 - The Second Brain

### First Article

**Title:**  
**The Road to Truly Intelligent Systems - The Second Brain: Why AI Needs Memory With Structure**

### Thesis

A serious business AI system needs more than document search. It needs a source-bound, versioned, graph-backed memory that can distinguish facts, methods, references, project lessons, outdated claims, and approved assets.

### Consultry Angle

Consultry's Second Brain is not a generic wiki and not only vector search. It is a compiled memory layer:

```text
Raw Sources -> Parsed Corpus -> Observations -> Graph Assertions -> Memory Packs -> Work Artifacts
```

Core Consultry ideas:

- Memory nodes for documents, facts, methods, references, profile claims, brand rules, offer patterns, project lessons, tender criteria, source spans.
- Triples, property edges, and hyperedges.
- SourceBinding, approval state, validity, confidence, and supersession.
- Job-scoped `MemoryPack`s for the harness.
- Aurora remains source of truth for MVP; dedicated graph DB deferred.

Plain-language example:

> A consulting firm does not need an AI that can find an old proposal. It needs an AI that knows which part of that proposal is still approved, which reference can be used, which method was superseded, and which claim has evidence.

### Audience Problem

Many businesses think "upload documents to a chatbot" is a knowledge strategy. The post should explain why that breaks down when facts need provenance, recency, approval, and reuse.

### Research Questions

- Where does classic RAG help, and where does it fail?
- What problems do GraphRAG and hybrid RAG address?
- How do we explain graph-backed memory without sounding academic?
- How can Consultry differentiate "Second Brain" from a document repository?

## 8. Post Plan 5 - The Art of Learning

### First Article

**Title:**  
**The Road to Truly Intelligent Systems - The Art of Learning: Why AI Systems Need Experience, Not Just Data**

### Thesis

The next useful AI systems will not simply know more. They will learn better from context, action histories, feedback, corrections, outcomes, and human approvals without blindly retraining on everything.

### Consultry Angle

Consultry's Win + Work loop can be explained as structured learning:

- In-context learning for immediate task adaptation.
- Corpus and MemoryPacks for reusable context.
- WorkAgent suggestions and confirmations for experience signals.
- ApprovalEvents and AuditRecords for reviewed feedback.
- Proposal edits and artifact acceptance as quality signals.
- Project lessons flowing back into the Second Brain.
- Careful distinction between memory, feedback, and model training.

Plain-language example:

> A consultant learns from projects, not only from documents. They remember what worked, what failed, what the partner corrected, which client objected, and which proof won trust. AI systems need a structured version of that loop.

### Audience Problem

People say AI "learns" when it often only adapts temporarily inside a context window. This post should explain the difference between in-context learning, memory, continual learning, and experience histories.

### Research Questions

- What is in-context learning and what are its limits?
- Why is continuous learning in LLMs hard?
- How can agents improve without updating model weights?
- What role do episodic memory, action history, reflection, and feedback play?
- How should a business system avoid uncontrolled learning from noisy or unsafe feedback?

### Source Plan

Primary research sources:

- **Language Models are Few-Shot Learners**  
  Use for in-context/few-shot adaptation without gradient updates.  
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165

- **What learning algorithm is in-context learning? Investigations with linear models**  
  Use for explaining ICL as an implicit algorithmic behavior.
